## Launch sequence

1. Lead with the public question. Record a short demo: inspect a disclosure and its boundary, change one hypothetical cooling input, reveal the electricity trade-off. Keep the illustrative banner visible.
2. Show a source locator and one limitation in the same post. Do not headline scenario reductions as achieved savings or accuse operators of causing a shortage.
3. Publish the release after checks pass. Link the demo, source register, case study and one good-first issue. Ask for a concrete review.
4. Share once in relevant communities under their self-promotion rules. Reply with evidence, correct mistakes publicly and credit contributors. Do not mass-message hiring managers.
5. Track actual sourced corrections, repeat users, successful reproductions and contributions. Stars/impressions are secondary; never fabricate engagement or testimonials.
6. For hiring conversations, demonstrate a definition rejection, SQL parity and an evidence gap. Explain your own choices and remaining work. Keep independent portfolio work separate from employment claims.

## Keep it useful

Review company reports annually and local evidence when it changes. Record review dates and hash drift. Triage corrections, maintain bounded contribution tasks, and publish an evidence-update note when verified coverage improves. Prefer a new local measurement over another unsupported dramatic claim.
